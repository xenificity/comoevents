Privacy Policy

CoMo Events, effective October 18, 2026, updated November 4, 2026 (Google and Facebook sign-in, photos, flyer scanning)

CoMo Events is a free community app made by an individual developer in Columbia, Missouri. We run no servers, show no ads, and include no third-party tracking SDKs. We count visits and taps anonymously, as described below.

What the app uses

  • Location. If you allow it, your location is used only to show where you are on the map and to center it. It is processed on your device and never sent anywhere.
  • Events feed. The app downloads a public list of events from GitHub (github.com/xenificity/comoevents). That request carries no personal information beyond what any web download does (your IP address, seen by GitHub).
  • Event links, images and directions. Opening tickets, event pages, venue images or directions sends you to that venue's site, the image host, or Apple Maps, which have their own policies.

Profiles and Sign in with Apple

Signing in is optional. If you sign in with Apple, the app stores your profile (display name, interests, who you usually go out with, and the ids of events you saved) in your own private iCloud database through Apple's CloudKit. Only you and your devices can read it; the developer has no access to it. Apple shares with the app only an anonymous user identifier and, on first sign in, the name you choose to share. We do not request or store your email address.

You can delete this data at any time from the Profile tab ("Delete my profile"), which removes it from your iCloud and from the device. You can also stop using Sign in with Apple for this app in your Apple ID settings.

Signing in with Google or Facebook

You can also sign in with Google or Facebook. You sign in on their own page; we never see your password. We receive your account id, name, email address and profile picture link. We keep your name, email address, an unreadable (hashed) account id, and your profile (display name, picture, interests, who you usually go out with and saved event ids) on our small server (a Cloudflare Worker) so it follows you to any device. Going, friends and posts you make with this account are stored in the same shared CloudKit database described below, under that hashed id.

You can delete all of this at any time: "Delete my profile" in the app or "Delete my account" on the website. Removing CoMo Events from your Facebook settings deletes it too.

Photos and scanned flyers

A profile picture, or a photo you add when posting an event, is uploaded to the same server. An event photo becomes public once a moderator approves the post, and is reviewed first. A profile picture is stored at a private, hard-to-guess address. When you scan a flyer, the text is read on your device (in the app) or in your browser (on the website); the image is uploaded only if you post the event with it. The app only sees photos you pick and uses the camera only when you scan.

What we collect

Besides the account data above, only what you choose to publish to other users. These optional features write to a shared (public) CloudKit database that the developer can read:

  • Community posts. When you post an event, the event details you type, your chosen display name and an anonymous iCloud user identifier are stored so the post can be shown to everyone, moderated, reported and blocked.
  • Attendance. Tapping Going, I'm here or Went stores that choice with the event id and your anonymous user identifier, so events can show how many people are going. No location is read or stored.
  • Usage count. The app and the web app store one anonymous "install" record each (a random id, the platform, the app version and two dates) so everyone can see how many people use CoMo Events. It is not linked to you, your device or your location.
  • Finding friends through Facebook or Google (optional). If you choose "Connect Facebook friends" or "Connect Google contacts" in Friends, you sign in on Facebook's or Google's own page. Our small server (a Cloudflare Worker) then reads your Facebook ID and the list of your Facebook friends who also connected CoMo Events, or your Google email address and the email addresses in your Google contacts. It uses them once to find people you know who already use CoMo Events, and adds those people to your Friends. We store only one record per connected account: a one-way scrambled code made from your Facebook ID or Google email with a secret key (it cannot be turned back into your ID or email), linked to your anonymous iCloud user ID. We never store your name from Facebook or Google, your friend list, your contacts or their email addresses, and we never post anything or contact anyone. Use Disconnect in Friends to delete that record; removing CoMo Events in your Facebook settings also deletes it. CoMo Events' use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
  • Anonymous visit and tap counts. The website and the app count page visits, app opens and taps such as opening an event, directions, saving or "Going", using GoatCounter. Each count records the page or action, the referring site or campaign, the browser or system, the screen size and the country or state derived from your internet address. GoatCounter sets no cookies, stores no internet address and assigns no identifier that follows you. The totals are public at comoevent.com/stats.
  • Friends. If you turn on Friends, a 6-letter code and your display name are stored, plus the ids of events you saved or are attending if you switch sharing on. Only people who have your code can see them.

Reports you file (reason and your anonymous identifier) are stored so a moderator can act on them. Nothing is used for advertising or tracking across apps and websites, and nothing is sold or shared with third parties except the anonymous counts sent to GoatCounter and the sign-in steps with Facebook or Google that you choose to start. Delete my profile in the Profile tab removes your profile and account; removing a post or turning off Friends removes those records. Contact picks for invitations stay on your phone.

Children

The app lists public community events, some at bars and breweries, and is rated accordingly. It is not directed at children under 13.
